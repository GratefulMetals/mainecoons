About sex and color: Maine Coons come in a very large number of color and pattern combinations. Nearly all of them occur in both males and females. Tortoiseshell, torbie, and calico patterns are the exception: because the genes for black-based and red-based pigment sit on the X chromosome, these patterns are overwhelmingly female. Males with them do occur but are rare and usually result from an unusual chromosome arrangement, and such males are typically sterile.

Reading a coat accurately: Part the fur to check the undercoat before deciding on a label. A white base with colored tips means smoke or silver rather than a true solid, and faint "ghost" striping in strong light is common in young red and cream cats that will still be registered as solids.
