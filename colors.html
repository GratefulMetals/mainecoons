How the looks developed

One breed, regional breeding emphasis

Modern Maine Coon lines have moved between North America, Europe, and Russia. In the late 20th century, American Maine Coons were imported into Europe, where some breeders selectively emphasized a more angular face, fuller square muzzle, and larger lynx tips. Later, Russian programs incorporated American and European stock and, in some local lines, continued selecting for those same traits with even greater emphasis.

That history describes a change in trait frequency within related Maine Coon bloodlines—not a different species or a guaranteed regional “type.” When breeders repeatedly choose cats with especially square, full muzzles or prominent lynx tips, those traits can become more common in a local line. The labels remain informal, and a cat’s appearance cannot prove its ancestry.

Important distinction: American, European, and Russian are informal look or line labels used by breeders and buyers. They are not separate Maine Coon breeds, and appearance alone cannot prove ancestry. Tortoiseshell, torbie, and calico patterns are female-biased because black- and red-based pigment are X-linked; rare males are possible.
How to verify a color: Ask the breeder for photos in natural light, part the coat to inspect the undercoat, and ask how the color is recorded on the pedigree. Red and cream often show ghost tabby markings; smoke must show a pale base at the root; a photo cannot establish genetic color by itself.
