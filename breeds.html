Pedigreed breed

Maine Coon

A long, rectangular, muscular cat built on a broad chest, with a squarish muzzle, high-set ears, and a shaggy uneven coat that is shorter over the shoulders and longer toward the rear.

  • Head: broad and slightly longer than wide, with a strong, blunt, square-looking muzzle
  • Profile: a gentle concave curve rather than a straight line
  • Ears: large, wide at the base, set high; lynx tips common but not required
  • Tail: long and flowing, roughly equal to body length
  • Coat: uneven shag with a full ruff, belly fur, and britches
Pedigreed breed

Norwegian Forest Cat

A powerful northern breed distinguished most reliably by its triangular head and dead-straight nose profile, carrying a dense water-repellent double coat.

  • Head: a clear equilateral triangle, more tapered than a Maine Coon's
  • Profile: a long straight line from nose tip to brow, with no break or dip
  • Eyes: large, almond-shaped, and set obliquely
  • Legs: hind legs noticeably higher than the front, raising the rump
  • Coat: glossy water-repellent guard hairs over a wooly undercoat
Coat category, not a breed

Domestic Longhair

"Domestic longhair" is a description of a mixed-breed cat with long fur, not a breed. Such a cat can be large, tufted, and impressive, and may closely resemble either pedigree breed without being one.

  • Build: highly variable, usually less consistently rectangular or heavy-boned
  • Head: variable, often more tapered or rounded than a Maine Coon's
  • Coat: any long-haired texture; frequently more even in length
  • Traits: ear tufts, big tails, and large size all occur in mixed-breed cats
  • Proof: without pedigree records, a visual match is not confirmation

Quickest single check: look at the cat in profile. A straight ruler-line from nose tip to forehead points toward a Norwegian Forest Cat, while a slight dip or concave curve points toward a Maine Coon. Combine that with muzzle shape, since the Maine Coon's blunt square muzzle is its most distinctive feature.
Responsible identification: Muzzle, ears, size, and coat cannot establish ancestry on their own, and many large fluffy cats in shelters are simply mixed-breed longhairs. If breed status matters for breeding, showing, insurance, or purchase price, ask for the documented pedigree rather than relying on photos or a breeder's description.

American, European & Russian Types

These labels describe informal breeding styles and regional trends, not separate breeds or official genetic divisions. Individual cats vary widely, and most modern pedigrees blend ancestry across countries.

Important context: There is no registry that splits the Maine Coon into "American," "European," or "Russian" breeds. All are the same breed judged against the same written standard. These terms are descriptive shorthand for a look, and in sales listings they are frequently used as marketing. Health testing, temperament, structure, and documented pedigree matter far more than the label.
Informal type label

American / "Sweet Type"

The traditional look closest to the original breed standard, generally read as softer and more balanced rather than deliberately exaggerated.

  • Expression: gentler and sweeter, often with rounder-looking eyes
  • Muzzle: strong and square but less dramatically projected
  • Ears: large and well-tufted, less vertically extreme
  • Coat: ranges from silky to moderately shaggy depending on the line
  • Reality check: "American" describes a style, not a genetic class
Informal type label

European / "Wild Type"

A more angular and dramatic interpretation, selected over recent decades for exaggerated versions of the same traits the standard already calls for.

  • Expression: more angular and intense, with pronounced cheekbones
  • Muzzle: typically longer, boxier, and more prominent
  • Ears: tall and upright with long lynx tips, often the signature trait
  • Eyes: may appear more oblique and deeper set
  • Reality check: exaggeration is a preference, and should never override structural soundness
Regional lineage label

Russian Line

A regional pedigree descriptor rather than a type of its own. Cats marketed as Russian line usually sit within the broader European dramatic-look trend, since Russian catteries have been influential in producing it.

  • Marketing emphasis: broad faces, strong cheekbones, heavy bone
  • Coat: dense, substantial coats are commonly highlighted
  • Variation: Russian breeders are not uniform in goals or quality
  • Price: the label is often used to justify a premium
  • Verification: judge the actual pedigree, testing, and contract, not the country
Common in modern breeding

Blended / International

Most working catteries now combine lines from several countries to balance health, genetic diversity, temperament, and type. This is the practical reality behind most pedigrees you will be shown.

  • Appearance: often a sweeter expression with larger ears or a stronger muzzle
  • Pedigree: may include American, European, and Russian ancestry within a few generations
  • Goal: standard-compliant structure and health rather than extremes
  • Ask for: multi-generation pedigree, cardiac screening, DNA panel results
  • Bottom line: choose the breeder and the individual cat, not the label
