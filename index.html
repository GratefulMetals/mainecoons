Step 2: Select Your Preferred Type

Maine Coons come in subtle regional variations.

American Type

Larger frame, muscular build, prominent ruff. Valued for size and classical Maine Coon appearance.

  • Weight: 13–18+ lbs
  • Head: Square, defined
  • Ears: Large with tufts
  • Body: Long, muscular

European Type

Leaner, refined build, finer bone structure. Emphasizes elegance alongside breed standards.

  • Weight: 11–15 lbs
  • Head: Refined contours
  • Ears: Proportionate to head
  • Body: Lean, athletic

Step 3: Key Health Priorities

Maine Coons have breed-specific health considerations. Review these before contacting a breeder.

Hypertrophic Cardiomyopathy (HCM)

Highest priority — affects up to 30% of Maine Coons.

What it is: Thickening of the heart muscle, reducing its ability to pump effectively.

Screening: Echocardiogram by a board-certified cardiologist. Repeat every 1–2 years.

Warning signs: Rapid/irregular heartbeat, lethargy, difficulty breathing, hind-leg paralysis (blood clot).

Ask breeders: "What is the cardiac history of both parents? When was each last screened? Do you have echocardiogram reports?"

Hip Dysplasia

Malformation of the hip joint; degree varies.

Screening: X-rays evaluated by PennHIP or OFA. Preferably before breeding.

Warning signs: Difficulty jumping, rear-leg weakness, limping, reduced activity.

Ask breeders: "Have both parents been hip-scored? What are the OFA or PennHIP results?"

Spinal Muscular Atrophy (SMA)

Rare but severe; kitten becomes progressively weak.

Genetics: Recessive — kitten must inherit the mutation from both parents.

Test: DNA test available; ethical breeders test parents.

Ask breeders: "Have both parents been DNA-tested for SMA? Can you provide the results?"

Polycystic Kidney Disease (PKD)

Cysts form in the kidneys; leads to kidney failure.

Genetics: Recessive — inherited from both parents.

Test: Ultrasound screening; DNA test for some lineages.

Ask breeders: "Have both parents been tested for PKD via ultrasound or DNA? What were the results?"

Veterinary Care Timeline

8–12 weeks Kitten arrival
  • First vet exam and vaccines (FVRCP series)
  • Parasite screening and prevention
  • Ask breeder for kitten's medical history
16 weeks Booster and neuter/spay
  • Final kitten vaccine booster
  • Feline leukemia (FeLV) test if outdoor access
  • Schedule spay/neuter if planned
1 year Adult baseline
  • Echocardiogram for HCM screening (first priority)
  • Annual wellness exam
  • Blood and urine baseline
  • Discuss dental care plan
2–7 years Prime adult
  • Annual wellness exams
  • Repeat echocardiogram every 1–2 years (HCM)
  • Monitor weight and activity
  • Watch for joint issues given large frame
7+ years Senior
  • Wellness exams every 6 months
  • Annual or biennial echocardiogram
  • Blood work to monitor kidney and thyroid function
  • Discuss pain management and quality-of-life planning
