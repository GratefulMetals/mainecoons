Visit & Cattery Details

Questions About the Parents

Health screening applies to the parents, so these answers matter more than any claim about a line or country of origin.

Have both parents had echocardiograms for HCM, and when was the most recent one?

Why it matters: DNA testing alone does not rule out HCM. Imaging by a qualified veterinary cardiologist is the primary screening method, and results expire in practical terms as the cat ages.

What DNA testing has been done on both parents, and at which lab?

Why it matters: Maine Coon panels commonly include SMA, PKDef, and the breed-specific HCM variant. Ask to see the certificates rather than a summary.

Have the parents' hips been evaluated, and what were the findings?

Why it matters: hip dysplasia is a recognized orthopedic concern in large, heavy-boned cats.

Was this a carrier-to-carrier pairing for any recessive condition?

Why it matters: two carriers of the same recessive condition, such as SMA or PKDef, can produce affected kittens.

May I see the multi-generation pedigree and any health history in the line?

Why it matters: pedigree documentation is what actually establishes ancestry claims, including any "European" or "Russian" line description.

Can I meet the mother, and see where the cats live?

Why it matters: the mother's temperament and the cleanliness and space of the environment tell you a great deal about socialization.

Has any cat in this line been diagnosed with HCM, SMA, PKDef, hip dysplasia, or other inherited disease?

Why it matters: an honest breeder discloses problems. A claim that nothing has ever occurred in the line is itself worth questioning.

Questions About Your Kitten

What vaccinations, deworming, and veterinary exams has this kitten already had?

Why it matters: you need the record to continue the schedule correctly with your own veterinarian.

Has this kitten had any illness, injury, or veterinary concern so far?

Why it matters: early issues shape your first vet visit and any follow-up plan.

Will the kitten be microchipped, and spayed or neutered before placement?

Why it matters: this affects registration terms, total cost, and your early veterinary scheduling.

Is the kitten sold as a pet or with breeding rights, and what registration papers come with it?

Why it matters: registration terms are the documented basis of any pedigree claim, and papers are sometimes withheld until proof of altering.

What food, litter, and daily routine is the kitten currently on?

Why it matters: keeping the routine consistent for the first weeks reduces stress and digestive upset during the transition.

How has the kitten been socialized with people, handling, noises, other pets, or children?

Why it matters: early socialization influences adult confidence far more than any regional type label.

What health guarantee, return policy, and ongoing support do you provide?

Why it matters: you want this in writing, including what happens if you can no longer keep the cat.

Your Assessment

Reasons to slow down: refusal to show pedigree or testing paperwork, a claim that a particular line needs no health testing, pressure for a fast non-refundable deposit, no written contract, unwillingness to let you see where the cats live, or an offer to release a kitten unusually early. Verify documents rather than relying on verbal assurances or photos.
